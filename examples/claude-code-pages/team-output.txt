examples/claude-code-pages/team.md:17:33  warning narrator/instruction-register
  > You paste it into your terminal, wait, and copy the answer back, and the team waits while you're in a meeting.
  why: A step said as an order ("You start a fresh model", "Paste the notes in"). In explaining prose it reads like an instruction manual; people talking say what you can do, or what they do.
  fix: Say "you can start...", "you could paste...", or what you do: "I usually start a fresh one", "we just paste it in". Real how-to steps belong in a numbered list, and a CTA such as "Try it" stays as it is.
  from: Founder edit of the lesson script, 2026-11-08: "You start a fresh model" became "You can start a fresh model"; a lot of AI writing reads like an order

# Ad landing page

`--page ad`, the default. A page that one search or one ad points to, such as a page for "claude code cloud".

## Goal

Turn a visitor with one pain into a sign-up with one promise. The page answers the search, shows the fix working and gives the visitor enough real content to believe a company stands behind it.

## Who arrives

A stranger from a search or an ad, with the other results open in tabs. They already live with the problem and know why it happens: that is why they searched. They want the fix and what it gets them. They leave when the page tells them what they already know, when it looks thin or fake, or when another tab shows the fix sooner.

## Structure

1. **Hero.** The title answers the search with the reader's gain ("Keep your Claude Code working when your laptop is shut"), a one-sentence subtitle, a call to action, and the full product with people and the agent working together, drawn like the main landing page.
2. **Problem.** A short title in the reader's words ("You've become the relay"), two or three sentences on what the problem costs them, an image beside the text. It reads like the opening of a blog post: plain, left-aligned, no button, no feature block.
3. **Transition.** A section that moves from the problem to the fix. If it compares the options people try, ours is framed as the answer, never one card in a row of look-alikes.
4. **Solution.** A section per gain, each with its own mock and a call to action every second section.
5. **Trust, setup, pricing, questions**, then a closing call to action.

## Rules

Every landing rule applies: `search-intent`, `thin-page` (700 words, 5 sections), `mock-per-claim`, `cta-along-page` (every second solution section), `problem-wall` (50 words), `problem-cta`, `known-mechanism`, `repeated-visual`, `crowd-title`, plus every ads rule.

## Checklist before the reader test

These need judgement, so no lint checks them. Read the page against each one once:

- Each section sells a concrete gain: something the reader can do now that they could not before. A feature restated is no gain.
- No section says what an earlier one said in other words.
- The page sells everything the reader gains beyond the obvious fix. On a page about an agent that runs on its own computer, that is the new ways of working once the agent needs no babysitting, on top of "it keeps running".
- The hero visual is the product in full, with people and the agent working together. A single chat bubble is too little.
- The problem part has no button and no card layout; buttons start with the solution.

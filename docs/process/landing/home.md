# Home page

`--page home`. The site's front page, such as laze.so. The most careful page on the site: every other page borrows its look and its claims.

## Goal

Tell any visitor in one screen what the product is, who it is for and what it changes for them, then show the product working in each of its main uses.

## Who arrives

Anyone who heard of the company: from a link, a colleague, an ad, the app store. Some know the category and some do not. They want to work out what this is and whether it is for them. They leave when they cannot say what it is after the hero.

## Structure

1. **Hero.** The one line the company stands on ("Running an AI native company is a team sport"), a short subtitle, a call to action, and the product in full.
2. **Problem**, short, if the page opens on one.
3. **Feature bands.** A band per main use: a short title, one or two sentences, a mock beside the text, sides alternating. A tour with tabs can hold several uses in one band.
4. **Pricing band** and a **closing call to action.**

## Rules

`thin-page` (500 words, 5 sections), `mock-per-claim`, `cta-along-page` (hero and end; buttons in between are allowed, not required), `problem-wall`, `problem-cta`, `known-mechanism`, `repeated-visual`, `crowd-title`, plus every ads rule. No `search-intent`: the home page answers the brand name.

## Checklist before the reader test

- After the hero, a stranger can say what the product is and who it is for.
- Each band shows a different use, each with its own visual.
- Approved lines stay as they are; a change to the home page is a change to every page that borrows from it.

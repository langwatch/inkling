# Stop

Stop editing when the core expectations pass. Simulated readers are generous, and fixing every small doubt past that point pushes copy back towards the average.

Every number in Inkling is a default, not a law: the 10% over budget, the six sentences without "we" or "you", two chunks at 2x. Reader tests give feedback to weigh, not a verdict. Tighten or loosen any of these for the piece in front of you and write down why.

Decide the time before you start: about 5 minutes for a short piece, 15 for a page or a post, 30 to 60 for a piece that matters. When the time runs out, ship the best version so far with a list of what is still open. See the [budget tool](../tools/budget.md).

Before shipping, give the result to readers who never saw an earlier version, once. If they pass the core expectations, ship. If not, make one small change and ship anyway: a second round would tune the draft to those readers too.

Keep what readers still asked. A question such as "Which teams run this?" asks for evidence the piece does not have yet. It goes into the lessons for the next piece on the same topic, so that piece starts further along.

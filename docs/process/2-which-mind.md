# Which mind

Pick the mind the reader needs. You write as a seller to make someone want something, as an engineer to make someone understand how it works, as a lawyer to leave no room for a second reading, and for an investor to show the numbers that matter. A brochure for a technical product needs a seller and an engineer at once.

Pick the mind for each reader from step 1 and write it next to them. Then pick the closest preset:

| Preset | For | Paragraph limit | What it asks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `docs` | product documentation | 80 words | strictly technical, no voice: present tense, tasks in the imperative, no marketing words |
| `post` | technical and founder posts | 80 words | an engineer talking to engineers: casual hedges allowed, no staccato or grand closing lines, every name introduced |
| `ads` | ads, landing pages, titles | 40 words | a business mind: lead with strengths, never concede or apologise, one claim per headline |
| `deck` | slides | 30 words | titles of six words or fewer, three bullets of two to four words, the story in the speaker notes |
| `legal` | order forms and terms | 120 words | exact periods and amounts, no "and/or" or "etc." |
| `investor` | investor updates and decks | 60 words | every number with its comparison, no hype words, no "we believe" |
| `x` | posts and replies on X | 50 words | the copy rules plus the post rules, and the first line counts as the title |
| `script` | narrated lessons and video scripts, heard once | 90 words | the post rules plus a time budget, an agenda, transitions and a teacher's voice; see [spoken scripts](script-mode.md) |

A preset only sets which rules apply. The reader line from step 1 stays free text, because no fixed list of audiences covers every piece.
